# Other Potential Angles: Analysis

**Status**: Brainstorming Paper Trail
**Date**: 2026-01-13
**Conclusion**: These are fine application areas but NOT compelling for Kickstarter. No true innovation beyond "Frontier has ODB + scripting."

---

## Why These Don't Work for Kickstarter

**What Kickstarter Needs**:
- True innovation (not "tool X could also do Y")
- Clear vision (pioneer a category, don't just serve a market)
- Emotional hook (people back the future, not features)
- Defensibility (why can't others copy this in 6 months?)

**What These Angles Are**:
- Derivative use cases
- "Frontier could be used for X" thinking
- Feature sets, not visions
- Copyable (no moat)

---

## The Angles (And Why They Fall Short)

### 1. Personal CRM / Relationship Management

**The Pitch**: ODB for contacts, scripts for follow-ups, AI summaries

**Why Not Compelling**:
- Not innovative (many CRMs exist, scripting alone isn't enough)
- No clear "aha!" moment
- Kickstarter backers won't get excited about "better contact management"
- Competition is fierce (HubSpot, Notion CRM, etc.)

**Verdict**: Fine feature, not a vision

---

### 2. Documentation / Technical Writing

**The Pitch**: Outline-based docs, ODB for metadata, multi-format publishing

**Why Not Compelling**:
- GitBook, ReadTheDocs, Docusaurus already exist
- "Better docs platform" isn't exciting
- Technical writers are small market
- No clear innovation beyond "it's scriptable"

**Verdict**: Nice-to-have, not pioneering

---

### 3. Data Journalism / Research Reporting

**The Pitch**: Data in ODB, outline for narrative, auto-update visualizations

**Why Not Compelling**:
- Jupyter + Python already serve this (well enough)
- Journalists are tiny market for fundraising
- Not clear why Frontier > Python notebooks
- No emotional hook

**Verdict**: Niche application, not category creation

---

### 4. Learning Management / Course Creation

**The Pitch**: Outline curriculum, ODB for students, scriptable automation

**Why Not Compelling**:
- LMS market is saturated (Canvas, Moodle, Teachable, etc.)
- "Flexible LMS" isn't innovative
- Educators don't back tech on Kickstarter in volume
- No clear advantage over existing tools

**Verdict**: Market exists, innovation doesn't

---

### 5. Small Business Operations Hub

**The Pitch**: All-in-one ODB for customers/invoices/inventory, scriptable workflows

**Why Not Compelling**:
- QuickBooks, FreshBooks, Wave already exist
- Small businesses want proven solutions, not new platforms
- "It's cheaper and scriptable" isn't exciting
- Huge support burden (every business has custom needs)

**Verdict**: Viable product, not Kickstarter material

---

### 6. API Documentation + Developer Portals

**The Pitch**: Interactive docs, generated from code, beautiful publishing

**Why Not Compelling**:
- Stripe docs are custom-built (expensive but works)
- Swagger/OpenAPI is good enough for most
- Small market (API-first companies only)
- Not clear why this beats existing solutions

**Verdict**: Nice feature for dev tools, not standalone vision

---

## The Pattern: Why None of These Work

**Common Problem**: They're all "Frontier could be used for X" thinking

**What's Missing**:
- No true innovation (ODB + scripting alone isn't enough)
- No category creation (just entering existing markets)
- No emotional hook (people don't get excited about better CRM)
- No defensibility (features can be copied)

**Contrast with AI-Augmented Knowledge Work**:
- ✅ True innovation (no one combines outline + ODB + MCP + publishing)
- ✅ Category creation (pioneering AI-augmented content like blogging in 1990s)
- ✅ Emotional hook (democratize content creation with AI)
- ✅ Defensible (architecture + heritage + timing)

---

## The Real Insight: Innovation is Rare

**Bad angles**: Apply existing platform to existing markets
- "Frontier for CRM"
- "Frontier for docs"
- "Frontier for X"

**Good angle**: Pioneer new category with unique architecture
- "Platform for AI-augmented knowledge work"
- Combines strengths: outline + ODB + scripting + MCP + publishing heritage
- Addresses emerging problem: AI content explosion needs organization
- Timing: Now (AI just hit mainstream)
- Heritage: Frontier pioneered blogging, can pioneer AI-augmented content

---

## What We Learned

**Lesson 1**: Platform thinking ("Frontier could do X") is lazy
- Every flexible platform can be used for many things
- Doesn't mean any of them are compelling

**Lesson 2**: Kickstarter needs vision, not features
- People back the future, not incremental improvements
- Need emotional story: "We pioneered blogging, now we'll pioneer AI-augmented content"

**Lesson 3**: True innovation is rare
- Most ideas are derivative ("X but with Y feature")
- Real innovation: New category, unique architecture, right timing

**Lesson 4**: Frontier's strengths are specific
- Outline-first (better for content than code)
- ODB (structured data for humans)
- Publishing heritage (RSS, blogging)
- MCP-native (AI integration)
- These strengths align with ONE clear vision: AI-augmented knowledge work

---

## Could Any of These Become Features?

**Yes, after establishing core vision**

**Phase 1**: Pioneer AI-augmented knowledge work (blogging, research, newsletters)
**Phase 2**: Community grows, people build on platform
**Phase 3**: Some of these emerge as community use cases:
- Someone builds CRM in Frontier (great!)
- Someone builds docs platform (awesome!)
- Someone builds course tools (cool!)

**But**: These are community-driven extensions, not the core vision

---

## The Right Question

**Wrong**: "What markets could Frontier serve?"
**Right**: "What category can Frontier pioneer?"

**Wrong**: "What features differentiate Frontier?"
**Right**: "What future can we create that others can't?"

**Wrong**: "Who would pay for Frontier?"
**Right**: "Who will be evangelists for Frontier's vision?"
